## The trademark trap — read first

**Never put an airline name in a domain.** Not `ryanairclaim.com`, not `easyjetdelay.com`, not as a subdomain. Airlines have active legal teams and UDRP is fast and cheap for them. You would lose the domain and every backlink pointing at it.

Airline names belong **in page titles and content** (nominative fair use — you are describing their service, not impersonating it), never in the domain.

Also avoid: "EU261" reads like an official body to a non-expert, and anything with "official", "authority", or "gov".

## Tier 1 — recommended

| Domain | Why |
|---|---|
| **amiowed.com** | Short, memorable, passes the radio test, and expands to class actions later |
| **delaypayout.com** | Keyword-adjacent, clearly descriptive, brandable |
| **flightdelaypay.com** | Most literal keyword match of the set |
| **claimfordelay.com** | Reads like the action the user wants to take |

## Tier 2 — fallbacks

- `delaycheck.com` — short, but ambiguous out of context
- `owedforflight.com`
- `threehourslate.com` — memorable, ties to the actual legal threshold
- `flightclaimcheck.com`

## Tier 3 — only if .com is gone

- `amiowed.co.uk` — genuinely worth considering *in addition*, since UK261 is half the market and a .co.uk ranks well for UK queries

## Recommendation

**amiowed.com** if available.

It is short, it is exactly the user's internal monologue, and it is the only candidate that also covers the class-action project — see the cross-project note in PORTFOLIO.md. If you ever consolidate those two, this domain is already the right home.

Fall back to **flightdelaypay.com** for the pure keyword play.

Budget $10–15/yr. Consider the `.co.uk` as a second registration given the UK traffic share.

## Don'ts

- Any airline name, in any form
- "EU261" or "official" or "authority"
- Hyphens, numbers
